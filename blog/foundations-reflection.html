Human Skills — Reflection

1. Three things I have learnt about myself and my ego

  • I can be reactive under pressure – I’ve noticed moments where my ego wants to defend itself instead of listening openly.
  • I rely more on validation than I realised – feedback, both positive and negative, impacts me strongly, and I need to learn to separate my self-worth from external opinions.
  • I grow most when uncomfortable – my ego resists challenge at first, but once I lean into discomfort, that’s when deeper learning happens.

2. The role of values, empathy, and self-awareness in learning and programming

  • Values provide an anchor: they guide how I work with others, what standards I uphold in my code, and how I handle ethical dilemmas in tech.
  • Empathy is vital in programming because software is for people – whether it’s understanding a user’s needs, supporting a team member, or writing clean code that others can understand.
  • Self-awareness helps me notice unhelpful patterns (like overcomplicating solutions or avoiding pair programming) and make conscious adjustments.

3. What has surprised me the most

I expected Human Skills to feel “soft” compared to coding, but I’ve been surprised at how practical they are. Group work, feedback sessions, and conflict resolution all directly affect how productive and enjoyable a coding project can be.

4. The most challenging aspects

  • Being vulnerable in front of peers – sharing personal reflections didn’t come naturally.
  • Receiving feedback without defensiveness – it’s hard to park the ego and simply listen.
  • Balancing honesty and diplomacy – learning to give feedback in a way that’s kind but still clear.

5. Why a programming school focuses on Human Skills

Because programming isn’t just typing code – it’s a team sport. Projects succeed or fail on communication, collaboration, and trust as much as technical ability. Employers hire people who can solve problems together, not just code alone.

I’ve worked in many development teams and the people I prefer to work with are not always the ones that have the most experience or technical skills; it’s the people with the best human skills.

6. Was it a waste of time?

Not at all. Practising only coding would have built technical skill, but not the ability to apply it effectively. Human Skills training complements programming by:

  • Making me a better collaborator.
  • Helping me manage stress and avoid burnout.
  • Teaching me to give and receive feedback productively.

I’ve always known from my previous experience of working in IT without technical skills that human skills are just as important.
