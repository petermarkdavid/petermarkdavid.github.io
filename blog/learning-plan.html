Bootcamp Learning Plan

Long-term goals and career pathway

My long-term goal is to learn more about programming and develop the skills to build my own projects. Having worked in software development for about 15 years, I want to be able to have higher-quality conversations with developers, grounded in my own hands-on coding knowledge. I see this as a way to open new opportunities—whether contributing to projects directly, prototyping ideas of my own, or bridging the gap between business and technical teams more effectively.

Using the skills I gain

The skills I gain at Dev Academy will allow me to build things myself rather than always relying on others. This is empowering, and I want to use that ability to explore creative ideas, side projects, and potentially more formal development work. Beyond the technical side, I also want to practice being a genuine contributor on a team, rather than only a manager or outsider. The impact I’d like to make is twofold: to build useful, well-structured projects of my own, and to bring a deeper level of empathy and understanding to the way I work with developers.

Strengths and limitations going into Bootcamp

My strengths include familiarity with development concepts and terminology from years of working alongside dev teams, as well as communication and empathy skills. I’ve also learned that I retain information best through conversation and collaboration.

My limitations include staying focused, being methodical enough to follow steps without jumping around, and occasionally waiting too long to ask for help. I can also get frustrated when working with people I don’t naturally connect with, so I want to work on patience and openness.

Re-evaluating strengths and limitations from Foundations

In Sprint 2, I lost more time than I should have with a technical issue—I should have asked for help earlier. The facilitator quickly unblocked me and gave me tips I wouldn’t have found otherwise, which reinforced the value of reaching out sooner. This is still a limitation I need to manage at Bootcamp. On the positive side, I’ve discovered that leaning into collaboration and pair programming feels more natural than I expected, which I now see as a strength to build on.

Biggest non-technical challenge at Bootcamp

My biggest non-technical challenge will be sustaining focus and energy across the nine weeks. I know that if I lose concentration, I can become scattered and less methodical. I’ll also need to manage my own balance of study with gym, life admin, and downtime to avoid burnout.

Human skills I’d like to develop

I’d like to strengthen my patience with people I don’t naturally connect with and become more effective at giving and receiving feedback. I also want to keep practicing collaboration as a contributor, not as a manager, and develop resilience when things don’t go my way. Improving my ability to timebox, ask for help earlier, and stay calm under pressure are also important growth areas for me.

Expectations of the Bootcamp team

I hope the facilitators will continue to provide one-to-one guidance when requested, and that they remain patient and calming, as I’ve already found David to be. For me, support looks like having approachable facilitators who help me see a clear way forward when I feel blocked or overwhelmed.

Expectations of myself

I expect myself to study and code for at least five hours per day during the week, with extra time at the weekend to finish sprints if needed. I will read shared blogs, learn from others, and encourage them. I’ll also take responsibility for timeboxing blockers, using the handbook or ChatGPT first, and then seeking help promptly—either from facilitators or peers when I feel comfortable.

Seeking help at Bootcamp

My plan is to first try to resolve blockers myself or with my pair/group. If I’m still stuck, I’ll use resources like the handbook and ChatGPT. If that doesn’t work, I’ll reach out to facilitators early, remembering the lesson from Foundations about not waiting too long.

Commitment to managing workload

I commit to managing my workload by balancing coding, rest, and life commitments. I will also be in the office on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, and will work from home on Thursday and Friday. I will communicate clearly with my team about this availability, while keeping it flexible if group needs require. I’ll manage myself to work productively and respectfully with other learners, facilitators, and industry/community representatives. My focus will be on contributing steadily, supporting others, and staying open to feedback and growth.
